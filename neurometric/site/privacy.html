Privacy

Collect the minimum, and only with consent

People who rely on assistive technology and BCIs often have no alternative way to use a computer. Measuring that use must never become surveillance of it.

This website

  • No cookies, local storage or session storage.
  • No analytics, trackers, advertising or third-party scripts.
  • No external fonts or embedded third-party content.
  • No forms. The only contact route is an email link you choose to use.

Whoever hosts the site may keep standard web-server access logs; that depends on the host, not on this site’s code.

The research software

The following describes how the NeuroMetric software is designed to handle data in a future research study. No participant data has been collected. A real study would also be governed by its IRB-approved protocol and consent form.

What may be collected, with consent

  • Task battery sessions: input events inside the battery (pointer positions and times, selections, key or switch presses on predefined tasks) and the scores computed from them.
  • Questionnaire answers: structured responses to instruments the study is licensed to use.
  • Aggregate home-use summaries: per period of up to 24 hours, active minutes, number of device sessions, counts of interactions by category, interruptions, and the number of text characters.
  • Self-reports: assistance received (type and minutes), fatigue and burden on 0–10 scales.
  • Optional, separately consented: minutes per coarse app category (such as “communication” or “media”, never app names), and free-text questionnaire answers.

What is never collected

  • Keystroke content or typed text outside predefined copy-typing phrases
  • Screenshots or screen content
  • Messages, emails or contacts
  • Browsing history, URLs, page or window titles
  • App names or bundle identifiers
  • Passwords or clipboard contents
  • Audio, location or raw neural signals

How the software enforces this

  • Opt-in, scoped consent. Consent is recorded per scope: task battery, questionnaires, home telemetry, app categories, free text, sharing with a study sponsor, and future research. Every scope is off unless the participant agrees to it. App-category minutes cannot be enabled without home telemetry.
  • Allowlist, not blocklist. Home-use summaries are checked against a strict allowlist of fields and value ranges. Unknown fields, content-like fields (text, URLs, titles, keystrokes, screenshots and similar), URL-like values and keystroke arrays are rejected. Rejected payloads are never stored; only a receipt with reason codes is kept.
  • Application-scoped by default. Telemetry covers the NeuroMetric application only. No operating-system-level monitoring ships enabled. Any future cross-application agent would require its own consent screen, a visible indicator, and pause and withdraw controls, and would never read key values or screen content or use accessibility services for monitoring.
  • Pseudonymous identifiers. Participants are identified in the data by study pseudonyms, not names.
  • Withdrawal and deletion. Participants can withdraw any scope; where withdrawal includes deletion, the data for that scope is deleted. Retention periods are enforced by a scheduled purge, and participant data can be exported on request.
  • Integrity. Stored records carry checksums, and changes to study records go to an audit trail.

Legal context, still to be reviewed

Whether health-privacy law (such as HIPAA) applies depends on who deploys the software and how. Several US states have added “neural data” to their privacy laws, and their definitions differ. NeuroMetric records interaction events rather than neural signals, but this has not been reviewed by counsel. Any study deployment will have its privacy terms reviewed by an IRB and legal counsel first.
