Collaborate

Help decide what should be measured, and test whether it works

NeuroMetric is at the stage where critical input matters most. We have no partners today, and we are looking for people willing to scrutinise the work.

Ways to get involved

Researchers

  • Critique the metric definitions, the simulator and its limitations.
  • Serve as, or connect us with, a faculty principal investigator for an IRB-approved pilot.
  • Test the battery with real switch interfaces, eye trackers, head pointers or BCIs in an existing research setting.
  • Suggest openly licensed datasets for reprocessing and benchmarking.

Assistive-technology clinicians and therapists

  • Tell us whether the tasks reflect the digital activities your clients care about.
  • Review the assistance categories and independence scale against clinical practice.
  • Advise on what home administration needs to look like to be feasible and respectful.

People who use BCIs or assistive technology, and care partners

  • Tell us what “meaningful benefit” means to you, and what should never be measured.
  • Review the privacy design and consent language before anything is used in a study.

Please do not send personal health information by email.

Device developers, study sponsors and funders

  • Discuss what neutral, home-use functional evidence would be useful for your programme.
  • Explore a scoped research pilot under an appropriate agreement and ethics review.
  • Research grant collaborations.

NeuroMetric is independent of all device makers, and any collaboration would be disclosed as such.

Questions we would especially like help with

  • Which everyday digital activities matter most to people who use BCIs or switches, eye gaze or head pointers?
  • How should assistance be recorded at home without adding burden to care partners?
  • Which established outcome instruments should be implemented, and under what licences?
  • What evidence of home use would researchers, clinicians and payers find credible from a neutral third party?
