## 1. Background
- People who cannot use a standard mouse or keyboard reach computers through assistive
  technology (AT): eye gaze, head pointers, joysticks, switch scanning, voice. In research
  settings, some use brain-computer interfaces.
- There is no openly specified, input-agnostic way to measure how well a person can do common
  digital activities with their access method. FDA guidance and field consensus documents call
  for validated, home-capable functional measures (`docs/research/regulatory_landscape.md`).
- NeuroMetric is an experimental, original browser-based task battery: pointing, grid
  selection, scrolling, drag, copy typing, and simulated digital activities. It is **not** a
  validated clinical outcome assessment, and it has no diagnostic purpose.

## 2. Objectives
- **Stage 1:** check task mechanics and feasibility in able-bodied adults.
- **Stage 2:** test whether scores distinguish input methods.
- **Stage 3:** estimate test–retest reliability and measurement error.
- **Stage 4:** estimate known-groups and convergent validity, floor effects and reliability in
  adults with ALS, SCI or stroke who use AT. Optional longitudinal follow-up.
- **Stage 5** (submitted later as an amendment): 2-week home-use feasibility with aggregate
  telemetry.

## 3. Design and procedures
Full detail is in `CLINICAL_VALIDATION_PLAN.md` and `docs/clinical/stage1_protocol.md` to
`stage5_protocol.md`. In summary:

| Stage | n | Visits | Time per participant |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 42 | 1 | about 75 min |
| 2 | 24 | 2 | about 150 min |
| 3 | 60 | 4 | about 170 min over ≤ 4 weeks |
| 4 | 45 | 3–4 (+ optional at 3 and 6 months) | about 4–5 h over 2–3 weeks, plus a 7-day care-partner diary |

Participants complete computer tasks with their usual input method, and short questionnaires
(NASA-TLX, SUS, original NeuroMetric items, and licensed instruments only if licences are
obtained). There is no deception. Sessions are not audio- or video-recorded.

## 4. Participants
- Adults ≥ 18 able to consent personally.
- Able-bodied adults (Stages 1–3).
- Adults with ALS, SCI or stroke who use AT for computer access (Stage 4).
- Care partners (Stage 4 diary; Stage 5).
- **Excluded:** minors; adults unable to consent personally (no LAR consent); prisoners; people
  with implanted BCIs (not recruited by this protocol).
- **Students:** UIUC students may take part as members of the public responding to flyers.
  No student records are used (`hipaa_ferpa.md`). The student investigator does not recruit
  classmates or people they supervise.

## 5. Recruitment
Flyers, email lists that accept research announcements (with list-owner permission), and
community and advocacy newsletters (flyer distribution only). Carle clinics only after the Carle
process. See `recruitment_materials.md`.

## 6. Consent
Written or electronic consent, obtained by a consent-trained team member who is **not** the
student founder. There is an accessible process for limited speech or hand function, including
an impartial witness for people physically unable to sign. No LAR. See the consent drafts.

## 7. Risks and benefits
See `risk_benefit_and_event_reporting.md`. Risks: fatigue, frustration, eye or neck strain,
discomfort from sitting, and a breach of confidentiality. There is no direct benefit.

## 8. Data
Pseudonymous IDs; university-hosted storage; no content capture; retention per the DMP. See
`data_management_plan.md`.
