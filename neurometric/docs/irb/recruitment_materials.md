# Recruitment materials

**DRAFT — not submitted — requires faculty PI and IRB review**

OPRS has guidance on recruiting by advertisements, emails and newsletters [V page exists:
https://oprs.research.illinois.edu/research-topics/recruiting-advertisementsemailsnewsletters-etc;
its content was not read this session]. All text below must be approved before use. Nothing may
promise benefit, use the word "free" for study procedures, or overstate compensation.

## 1. Accessible formats (all materials)
- Plain language, aiming for about grade 6–8 reading level [INF target]; short sentences.
- Large-print versions (≥ 18 pt sans-serif, high contrast).
- Tagged, screen-reader-accessible PDF and HTML. Every image has alt text. Text is never
  embedded only in images.
- Easy-read summary with one idea per line.
- Can be loaded onto AAC devices on request. Email replies are accepted by any method (AAC-typed
  email, care partner typing on the person's behalf with their permission, phone, video relay).
- QR codes are always paired with a typed short URL and a phone/email option.
- Captioned video version of the flyer, if made later, requires a separate IRB approval.

## 2. Flyer: able-bodied adults (Stages 1–3)
> **Research study: how people use computers**
> We are testing new computer tasks (pointing, typing, and simple practice activities like
> finding a contact).
> **Who:** adults aged 18–65 who use a computer mouse every day and have no condition that
> affects how they use a computer.
> **What:** 1 to 4 visits to a campus lab, about 40–75 minutes each, depending on the study part.
> **Payment:** *[amount per visit, as approved]*.
> **Contact:** *[STUDY EMAIL]* · *[STUDY PHONE]* · *[short URL]*
> Principal Investigator: *[FACULTY PI NAME, DEPARTMENT]*, University of Illinois
> Urbana-Champaign. IRB # *[number]*.
> Taking part is voluntary.

## 3. Flyer: assistive-technology users (Stage 4)
> **Research study: measuring computer use with assistive technology**
> Do you use eye gaze, a head pointer, a joystick, switches, voice, or another method instead of
> a standard mouse or keyboard? Do you have ALS, a spinal cord injury, or a stroke?
> We are testing new computer tasks that work with many access methods. We want to learn whether
> they measure computer use fairly and consistently.
> **What:** 3–4 sessions (about 60–90 minutes each, with breaks) at a campus lab **or at your
> home**, using your own device and settings. A family member or care partner is welcome. With
> their agreement, they may fill in a 2-minute daily form for one week.
> **Payment:** *[amounts]*; travel costs reimbursed.
> **This study does not provide treatment or new equipment.**
> **Contact:** *[STUDY EMAIL]* · *[STUDY PHONE]* · *[short URL]*. Contact us by email, text-to-speech,
> or through someone you choose.
> Principal Investigator: *[FACULTY PI NAME]*, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. IRB # *[number]*.

## 4. Email or newsletter text
> Subject: Research study: computer tasks for people who use assistive technology (University of Illinois)
>
> The *[LAB NAME]* at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign is looking for adults with ALS,
> spinal cord injury or stroke who use assistive technology to use a computer or tablet. You
> would complete computer tasks with your own device, in our lab or at home, in 3–4 sessions.
> Payment: *[amount]*. Taking part is voluntary. It will not affect your care or services.
> Details: *[short URL]*. Contact: *[STUDY EMAIL]*.
> IRB # *[number]*. PI: *[FACULTY PI NAME]*.

List owners distribute the message themselves. The study team does not receive member lists.
Organizations that only distribute flyers are not engaged in research, provided they do not
answer study questions or take part in consent [V, OPRS non-Illinois researchers page].

## 5. Phone, video or in-person screening script (study staff, not the founder)
1. "Thank you for your interest. My name is *[name]*, and I work with *[PI]* at the University of
   Illinois. Is now a good time? We can also do this by email or text, or another day."
2. "I'll briefly describe the study, then ask a few questions to see if you're eligible. You can
   stop at any time. If you're not eligible, I'll delete your answers unless you ask us to
   contact you about future studies."
3. Description (from the flyer).
4. Screening questions:
   - age ≥ 18;
   - for Stage 4: diagnosis (self-report); access method; device availability; whether a session
     of 60–90 minutes with breaks is possible; preferred setting (lab or home); communication
     preferences;
   - for Stages 1–3: daily mouse use; no condition affecting computer use; previous NeuroMetric
     participation.
5. If eligible: "We'll send you the consent information in *[format]* at least 3 days before your
   first session, so you have time to read it and ask questions."
6. If not eligible: thank them. Do not record identifiers unless they consent to a future-contact
   list.

**AAC users:** questions are asked one at a time, yes/no where possible. Wait time is unlimited.
Email screening is an equal alternative.

## 6. Study web page text
The flyer content, plus the PI and IRB contact, a summary of "what we collect and don't collect"
(from `consent_home_telemetry.md` §3 for Stage 5), and accessibility statements. **No company
branding or product marketing on recruitment pages** (COI plan).

## 7. Not permitted
- The founder recruiting in their classes, clubs, residence hall or employment settings.
- Mass email using student records. FERPA applies, and the IRB cannot waive FERPA consent [V].
  Campus mass-email channels have their own approval rules [UNR].
- Social-media posts by the company account. Recruitment posts may come only from the lab or
  study account, with approved text.
