# Consent document: adults who use assistive technology (Stage 4; Stage 5 adds `consent_home_telemetry.md`)

**DRAFT — not submitted — requires faculty PI and IRB review**

The final form uses the OPRS "Consent Document" template. Use the "Consent and Authorization
Document" if protected health information is obtained from a HIPAA-covered entity
(`hipaa_ferpa.md`). The OPRS **Witness Signature Page** is used where §B.5 applies [V templates
listed on https://oprs.research.illinois.edu/research-topics/consent-templates].
Assent is not applicable. **LAR consent is not used.** Adults who cannot consent personally are
not enrolled.

## A. Consent form text (plain language; also given as an easy-read summary)

**University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign: Research Consent**
**Study:** Measuring computer use with assistive technology
**Principal Investigator:** *[FACULTY PI NAME]*, *[DEPARTMENT]*, *[PI CAMPUS EMAIL]*, *[PI PHONE]*
**IRB #:** *[number]*. **Version / date:** *[v, date]*

### Key information
- This is a research study. Taking part is **your choice**. Saying no will not change your health
  care, your services, or your access to any equipment.
- We are testing new, experimental computer tasks that work with many ways of using a computer:
  eye gaze, head pointer, joystick, switches, voice and others. We want to learn whether the
  tasks measure computer use fairly and give similar results on different days.
- **This study does not treat you. It does not give you new equipment. Your scores are not
  medical results.**
- **Time:** 3 sessions (a 4th is optional), each up to 60–90 minutes with breaks, over about 2–3
  weeks. You choose a campus lab or your home, and we keep the same place for all sessions.
  Optional follow-up sessions at 3 and 6 months.
- **Risks:** tiredness; eye, neck or body discomfort; frustration; a small privacy risk.
  - *For people with spinal cord injury at T6 or above:* any session can, rarely, trigger
    autonomic dysreflexia. We follow your own plan, and we stop immediately if you have symptoms.
- **Benefits:** none directly.
- **Payment:** *[amount]* per session; travel costs; *[care-partner amount]*.
- **Financial interest:** a student on the team founded a company that may sell this software.
  Details are below.

### What will happen?
- **Session 0 (practice):** we talk about the study and this form, record how your device is set
  up (settings, mounting, switch position), and you practise the tasks. Photos are taken **of
  the equipment only**, never of you, and only with your permission.
- **Sessions 1 and 2 (and optional 3):** you do the same computer tasks with your own device and
  settings, 3–7 days apart, plus short questionnaires about effort, ease of use and how you use
  technology. Tasks include: selecting targets; choosing squares on a grid; scrolling; moving an
  object; copying short phrases on an on-screen keyboard; practice activities such as sending a
  pretend message (nothing is really sent).
- **Breaks:** at least every 15 minutes of tasks, and whenever you want. We agree a "stop" signal
  with you at the start.
- If you get very tired, we stop and finish another day (within 2 days).
- **With your agreement,** a care partner fills in a 2-minute form each day for one week about
  help with computer and daily tasks. They sign their own consent form.

### What information is collected?
**Collected:**
- pointer and selection timing **within the study tasks**;
- your task scores;
- your access method and settings;
- questionnaire answers;
- your diagnosis, time since diagnosis or injury, age range and how you use technology (as you
  tell us);
- notes when someone helps.

**Not collected:**
- audio or video recordings;
- photos of you;
- where your eyes look (eye-gaze coordinates);
- anything you do outside the study tasks;
- your own messages or text.

We will ask a doctor or clinic to confirm your diagnosis **only if** you give separate written
permission.

### Risks and how we reduce them
- Tiredness or discomfort: regular breaks; stopping at any time; your positioning and pressure
  relief routine.
- Spinal cord injury at T6 or above: we record your autonomic dysreflexia plan before starting,
  and staff are trained to recognize signs.
- ALS: we follow your breathing routine and stop whenever you ask.
- Privacy: code numbers; secure university storage; limited access.
- Frustration: the tasks are designed so that some will be hard for everyone. Difficulty is
  expected and is not a failure.

Same text as the able-bodied consent, adapted to plain language. The student founder will not
obtain your consent or run the main analysis.

## B. Accessible consent process (procedure for staff)
1. **Before the visit.** Send the form at least 3 days ahead in the format the person chooses:
   accessible PDF/HTML (screen reader and switch navigable), large print, easy-read summary, or
   pages for their AAC device.
2. **Pacing.** No time limit. The discussion may be split across visits. Allow processing time
   for every answer (AAC users may need minutes per sentence).
3. **Communication methods accepted:**
   - the person's AAC device;
   - eye-gaze board;
   - partner-assisted scanning with a confirmed yes/no signal;
   - consistent eye-blink or eye-movement yes/no, checked first with 3 neutral practice
     questions (e.g. "Is your name *[name]*?", "Are we in *[city]*?", "Is it raining in this
     room?");
   - speech;
   - writing.
   The communication method is documented on the consent record.
4. **Independence of the decision.** The care partner may support communication. Staff ask each
   key question directly to the participant and confirm the response through the participant's
   own signal. If staff believe a response may not be the participant's own, the consent
   discussion pauses.
5. **Comprehension check (teach-back; original items; yes/no or multiple choice):**
   1. "Is this study a treatment for your condition?" (No)
   2. "Can you stop at any time without it affecting your care?" (Yes)
   3. "Will we record video of you?" (No)
   4. "About how many sessions are there?" (3, or 4 if you choose the optional one)
   5. "If you get tired, what can you do?" (Signal stop / take a break)

   ≥ 4/5 correct after up to two explanations is needed to proceed. Not reaching it is handled
   respectfully. The person is thanked, and no data are retained.
6. **Documentation of consent:**
   - (a) **Signature by hand or by mark**, with assistance to hold the pen if requested; or
   - (b) **Electronic signature** through an IRB-approved system with a verified identity. OPRS:
     "To be legally effective, the electronic signature needs to be attributable to a verified
     identity" [V]
     https://oprs.research.illinois.edu/research-topics/electronic-signatures-and-waivers-documentation-consent.
     A switch- or gaze-accessible signing step is acceptable if the system verifies identity
     [INF; IRB decides]; or
   - (c) **Witnessed consent** for a person physically unable to sign. The participant expresses
     consent through their communication method in the presence of an **impartial witness**:
     preferably someone not on the study team and not the care partner, if available. The
     witness completes the OPRS Witness Signature Page, attesting that the information was
     presented, the participant's questions were answered, and consent was freely given by the
     participant's own expression. The consent-taker documents the method used.
7. **No LAR.** If the person cannot give consent themselves, they are not enrolled. OPRS refers
   to additional safeguards for adults with impaired decision-making capacity [V]
   https://oprs.research.illinois.edu/research-topics/vulnerable-populations. Illinois rules on
   research surrogates are not relied on [UNR].
8. **Ongoing consent.** Re-confirm willingness at the start of every session with the yes/no
   method. For longitudinal ALS visits, repeat the teach-back check briefly. If capacity appears
   to have changed, stop participation. Already collected data are handled per the participant's
   recorded choice.
9. **Copies.** A copy is given in the participant's preferred format.
