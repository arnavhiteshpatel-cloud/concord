# Customer-discovery interview guides

Prepared 2026-11-27. Seven guides, one per interviewee type. They are written for the prospects in `prospects.md`
and the evidence gaps described in `neuromodulation_market.md`. No interviews have been run; nothing here is a finding.
All statements about what interviewees "will" say are hypotheses [HYP].

## 0. Rules for every interview (Mom-Test discipline)

1. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time..." beats "Would you use...". Opinions about
   hypothetical products are not data.
2. **Do not pitch, and do not name NeuroMetric's features before the last five minutes.** If the interviewee asks what we
   are building, say we are studying how teams measure function and real-world use, and offer to explain at the end.
3. **Look for money, time and workarounds already spent.** A problem nobody has spent money or time on is not a problem worth
   building for.
4. **Get specifics:** which study, which instrument, which payer, who did the work, how many hours, what it cost, what happened next.
5. **Never ask leading questions** ("Wouldn't it be helpful if..."). Never ask for a commitment in the first conversation
   except a concrete next step (introduction, document, follow-up date).
6. **Confidentiality.** Do not ask for unpublished trial data, patient data or confidential payer correspondence. If offered,
   decline anything identifiable. Record only what the interviewee agrees can be noted.
7. **Neutrality boundary.** NeuroMetric never controls devices or changes stimulation. If a conversation drifts toward device
   control or closed-loop therapy, note it as a signal but do not explore building it.
8. **Recruiting.** Use only public routes listed in `prospects.md` (company contact pages, IR pages, LinkedIn company pages,
   registry contacts, conference introductions). Do not invent or scrape personal contact details.

**Standard opening (all guides, ~2 min):** purpose (learning how teams measure function, real-world use and care-partner
impact for neurotechnology), 30-45 minutes, no sales, notes kept without attribution unless agreed, they can skip any question.

**Standard close (all guides):** "What should I have asked that I didn't?" / "Who else should I talk to about this?" /
"Is there a document you could share that shows how you do this today (a protocol synopsis, a payer submission outline, a CRF)?"
Only then, if appropriate, a 2-minute description of what NeuroMetric is exploring and "Would it be useful to see a draft
in [timeframe]?" (record the exact answer and any concrete next step).

**After-interview scoring (fill within 24 h):** pain intensity (0-3), evidence of spend (none / time / money / both),
current solution (none / spreadsheet / vendor / in-house system), decision-maker identified (y/n), next step agreed (y/n), and
the single most surprising thing said.

## 1. BCI sponsors - clinical and regulatory leads

**Who:** VP Clinical Affairs, Head of Regulatory, Clinical Program Manager at implantable or non-invasive BCI companies
(e.g., Synchron, Paradromics, Neuralink, Cognixion, Precision, Blackrock).

**What we want to learn**
- How they choose functional and real-world-use endpoints as they move from early feasibility to pivotal.
- What FDA has said (in pre-subs, IDE feedback, workshops) about clinical benefit and COAs, as far as they can share.
- Who collects and analyzes home-use data today, with what tools, and how much effort it takes.
- Whether care-partner burden is measured, and why or why not.
- Whether a neutral third party adds credibility or creates risk in their view.

**Questions**
1. Walk me through how the endpoints for your most recent study were chosen. Who was in the room, and what options were dropped?
2. What did you hear from FDA about demonstrating clinical benefit, beyond safety? What changed in your plan afterwards?
3. Think of the last participant who used the system at home. What do you actually know about how they used it day to day, and how do you know it?
4. Who on your team, or which vendor, handles home-use data today? Roughly how many hours a week does it take?
5. Tell me about a time a measure you collected turned out to be hard to interpret or defend. What happened?
6. Which validated instruments have you tried (for example PIADS, QUEST, NASA-TLX, COPM, ZBI)? Which ones did participants or sites push back on?
7. How do you handle participants whose disease progresses during the study, for example in ALS? What did you do last time?
8. Have care partners been part of any outcome you measure? If not, how was that decided?
9. When you compare your system to what participants already use (eye-gaze, switches, voice), how do you do that today?
10. What have you built in-house for outcome measurement, and what would you never build in-house again?
11. Tell me about the last time you brought in an outside vendor for any part of trial measurement. How was that decided, and who signed off?
12. How are you following the iBCI Collaborative Community or other consensus work on endpoints? Has it changed anything you do?
13. What is the next decision on endpoints or measurement you need to make, and by when?

**Disconfirming signals (would lower fit or urgency)**
- "Endpoints are already locked for pivotal and we're happy with them."
- Home-use data already flows automatically from their own software into analysis, with a named owner and no complaints.
- No interest in care-partner measures, and a clear rationale (e.g., FDA indicated it is not needed).
- Any third-party involvement is seen as an unacceptable data-integrity or IP risk.
- No budget line or decision-maker for measurement outside the CRO contract.

## 2. BCI market access and HEOR leads

**Who:** Head of Market Access, HEOR lead, Reimbursement director at BCI companies, or the person who owns payer
strategy if there is no such role yet (often a CEO or CMO at early companies).

**What we want to learn**
- Whether anyone owns payer evidence yet and when they expect to need it.
- Which payers or coverage pathways they are planning for (Medicare, VA, commercial, outside US) and what those payers asked for.
- What evidence gaps they already see between their trial data and what payers will ask for.
- How they think about value: participant independence, care-partner time, healthcare utilisation, employment.

**Questions**
1. Who in the company owns the question "how will this be paid for"? When did that start?
2. Tell me about the last conversation you had with a payer, a Medicare contractor or a health-technology assessment body. What did they ask about?
3. What data from your current studies do you expect payers to find least convincing? Why?
4. When you've built a value story so far, which outcomes did you put first, and where did the numbers come from?
5. Have you tried to measure care-partner time or burden? What happened?
6. What do you know today about healthcare use or support costs before and after implant for your participants? How did you get it?
7. Which comparator do you expect a payer to use: current AAC/AT, standard care, nothing? What evidence do you have against that comparator?
8. Tell me about a case where an adjacent device (for example an SGD, a power wheelchair, a neurostimulator) got coverage or was denied. What did you learn from it?
9. Have you budgeted for real-world evidence after approval? What is in that plan today?
10. Who would you need to convince internally to fund an evidence program that is not part of the pivotal trial?
11. What have consultants or HEOR vendors done for you so far, and what did you think of the output?
12. What would make you trust outcome data that your own company did not collect? What would make you distrust it?

**Disconfirming signals**
- No one owns payer evidence and the company does not expect to think about it before approval.
- They plan to rely entirely on a single consultant model built from trial data, with no post-market data plan.
- Payers they have spoken to asked only about safety and price.
- Belief that coverage will come via policy advocacy alone, without new evidence.

## 3. Rehabilitation and neuromodulation device manufacturers

**Who:** VP Clinical, Head of Market Access, HEOR, Product lead at ONWARD, Bioness, Kandu, Mobia, Myomo, Cala and similar.

**What we want to learn**
- What happens between clinic visits and during home-use phases, and what they can see of it.
- Which payer denials or prior-authorization requests were caused by missing evidence, and what evidence was missing.
- Whether their device logs are exportable, who owns them, and whether they would share them read-only.
- How they run registries and post-market studies today, and with whom.

**Questions**
1. Think of a patient who started home use in the last few months. What do you know about what they did at home, and where does that information come from?
2. What does your device or app record about sessions? Who looks at it, and how often?
3. Tell me about the most recent prior-authorization denial or appeal you were involved in. What did the payer ask for that you could not provide?
4. When clinicians write letters of medical necessity for your device, what evidence do they cite? Who drafts them?
5. Which outcome measures do clinics actually collect for your patients outside trials? How consistent is that across sites?
6. How is your registry or post-market study run today: who collects data, who pays, what is the follow-up rate?
7. What did your last real-world evidence effort cost, roughly, and what did you get for it?
8. Have you tried to measure real-world use with wearables or sensors separate from the device? What happened?
9. Has caregiver involvement or burden come up with payers, clinics or patients? In what way?
10. Where would you draw the line on a third party touching your device data? What has legal or quality said about this before?
11. Who would decide whether to add a measurement component to an ongoing study or registry, and how long would that take?
12. If you look at the next 12 months, which evidence milestone matters most to the business, and why?
13. What has a competitor done on evidence that you noticed?

**Disconfirming signals**
- Reimbursement is stable and no recent denials cite evidence gaps.
- The device app already captures adherence and outcomes that payers accept, with a named team.
- Device logs cannot leave the device or will never be shared, and no workaround (separate sensors, PROs) is acceptable.
- Evidence budget is fully committed to an existing CRO or registry vendor for multiple years.

## 4. Clinical researchers and principal investigators

**Who:** PIs and research operations leads at BrainGate sites, Pitt/UChicago, UC Davis, JHU, Caltech/Rancho, UCSF,
Miami, EPFL/.NeuroRestore, UMC Utrecht, Clinatec, U Calgary, VA Palo Alto, Shirley Ryan AbilityLab.

**What we want to learn**
- The real burden of collecting functional, home-use and care-partner outcomes in small-n, long-duration studies.
- Which instruments they trust, which they abandoned, and why.
- Whether cross-site harmonisation is wanted, and who would pay for it (grant, sponsor, institution).
- Data-governance constraints (IRB, data use agreements, sponsor ownership).

**Questions**
1. Describe a typical week of outcome collection for one participant. Who does what, and how long does it take?
2. Which instruments did you choose for your current protocol, and which did you consider and reject? Why?
3. Tell me about the last time a participant used the system at home without staff present. What do you know about that session?
4. What do you do when an instrument doesn't fit a participant, for example floor effects in severe tetraplegia or progression in ALS?
5. How do you currently capture input from family or care partners, if at all?
6. Have you tried to compare results with another site or study? What got in the way?
7. Tell me about the last time an IRB, data-use agreement or sponsor contract limited what you could collect or share.
8. Which tools or software do you use for outcomes now (REDCap, custom scripts, vendor eCOA)? What do you dislike about them?
9. How are outcome-measurement costs funded in your current grant or sponsor agreement? Was anything cut?
10. If you are involved in iBCI-CC, FDA/NIH workshops or other consensus efforts, what has been hardest to agree on?
11. What would a measure need to show for you to add it to your next protocol?
12. Who else in your group or institution would be part of a decision to add a measurement component?

**Disconfirming signals**
- Measurement burden is described as manageable and staffed; no workarounds in use.
- Strong preference for fully in-house tools for scientific control, with no interest in harmonisation.
- No budget mechanism for anything beyond existing grant lines, and no plan to seek one.
- Data cannot leave the institution in any form, including de-identified summaries.

## 5. Assistive technology clinicians (OT, SLP, ATP)

**Who:** occupational therapists, speech-language pathologists, assistive technology professionals and seating/mobility
specialists who assess, prescribe and fund AAC, computer access and rehab devices, including in SCI, ALS and stroke programs.

**What we want to learn**
- How they document need and benefit for funding (SGD reports, LMNs, re-evaluations) and how long it takes.
- What evidence funders accept or reject, and where devices are abandoned after funding.
- Which outcome measures they use in practice, versus in research.
- Their view of BCIs as an access method and what they would need to see to recommend one.

**Questions**
1. Walk me through your last funding report for an AAC or access device. What did you include, and how long did it take?
2. Tell me about the last funding request that was denied or delayed. What did the funder say was missing?
3. Which outcome measures do you actually use in clinic for AT (for example COPM, GAS, QUEST, PIADS)? When did you last use each?
4. After a device is delivered, how do you find out whether it is being used? What did you learn the last time you checked?
5. Tell me about a client who stopped using a device. When and how did you find out, and why did it happen?
6. How do care partners figure in your assessment and follow-up? Is their time or strain documented anywhere?
7. When a client's abilities change (for example ALS progression), what triggers a re-evaluation, and who pays for it?
8. Have you assessed or worked with anyone using a BCI, or been asked about one? What happened?
9. How do you compare access methods for a client today (eye-gaze vs switches vs head mouse)? What do you record?
10. What software or forms do you use for documentation, and what do you wish they did differently?
11. Who in your organisation decides which assessment tools are adopted?
12. If a manufacturer gives you outcome data about their device, how much do you trust it, and why?

**Disconfirming signals**
- Funding documentation is quick, templated and rarely challenged.
- Post-delivery use is already tracked by a tool they are satisfied with.
- Clinicians see no role for standardized measures beyond what funders mandate.
- Organisation forbids any third-party tool in documentation workflows.

## 6. Payer evidence specialists

**Who:** medical-policy analysts, technology-assessment staff, DME/O&P coverage reviewers at commercial plans, Medicare
contractors (where they will speak), VA prosthetics and sensory aids staff, and HTA bodies outside the US. Interview as
learning conversations only; do not discuss any specific pending claim.

**What we want to learn**
- What evidence moves a neurotechnology device from "investigational" to covered, in their own past decisions.
- How they weigh real-world-use data, patient-reported outcomes and care-partner burden relative to RCT impairment scores.
- Whether independence of the data collector from the manufacturer matters, and how they judge it.
- Where policy documents (e.g., Anthem DME.00052 for BCI rehabilitation) came from and what would trigger a re-review.

**Questions**
1. Think of a device policy you worked on that changed from investigational to covered. What evidence made the difference?
2. When you review a device that is used at home, what do you look for about actual use and adherence? Where have you seen it done well?
3. How do you treat outcome data collected by the manufacturer versus by an independent party? Can you recall a case where that mattered?
4. Which outcome measures carry weight with your clinical reviewers for stroke or spinal-cord-injury function? Which do they discount?
5. Tell me about a time patient-reported or caregiver-reported outcomes influenced a decision, or failed to.
6. What do you need to see about durability after treatment ends? How long is long enough in your recent decisions?
7. How do registries or post-market studies factor into re-reviews? What made one credible or not?
8. What triggers a re-review of an existing policy? How often does that happen in practice?
9. When evidence is mostly single-arm or small, what, if anything, can make it acceptable?
10. How do you think about cost offsets such as care-partner time, home-care hours or hospital use? Have you ever used them?
11. Which sources do you rely on for technology assessment (internal staff, external HTA vendors, specialty societies)?
12. Is there anything manufacturers routinely send you that you never read?

**Disconfirming signals**
- Only RCT evidence with impairment endpoints counts; real-world or PRO data are disregarded.
- Independence of the data collector makes no difference to them.
- Care-partner burden and cost offsets are out of scope for coverage decisions in their organisation.
- Policies are re-reviewed only on a fixed cycle regardless of new evidence.

## 7. Clinical research software companies (partnership and competition check)

**Who:** product, partnerships or scientific leads at eCOA/ePRO vendors, decentralised-trial platforms, wearable-endpoint
companies, registry platforms and EDC providers that serve medtech or neurology. Treat as a peer conversation; be open that
NeuroMetric is exploring adjacent territory, and do not ask for confidential roadmaps.

**What we want to learn**
- Whether they already serve BCI, neuromodulation or AT sponsors, and with what (instruments licensed, sensors, analytics).
- What they decline to do (custom device-log ingestion, small-n studies, caregiver modules, payer packaging).
- How they validate digital measures and handle COA licensing.
- Whether partnership (they host, we provide measures or analytics) or competition is more likely.

**Questions**
1. Which neurology or medtech device studies have you supported in the last two years? What did sponsors ask for most?
2. Have you worked with brain-computer interface, neuromodulation or assistive-technology sponsors? What was different about them?
3. Tell me about a request from a sponsor that you turned down. Why?
4. How do you handle device-generated data from a sponsor's own product? Can you recall a project where that went badly?
5. Which COA instruments do you license most often for neurology? Have you implemented PIADS, QUEST, COPM, NASA-TLX or ZBI?
6. How have you validated a digital or sensor-based measure for a regulatory submission? How long did it take, and who paid?
7. Do you support care-partner reported outcomes as a separate respondent? What issues came up?
8. How small a study is still viable for you commercially? What happens below that size?
9. How do your sponsors use your outputs for payers or HTA, if at all?
10. Where do you see consolidation or new entrants in this area?
11. What kinds of partners do you integrate with today, and what did the last integration cost on your side?
12. If a sponsor asked for an independent, device-agnostic functional measurement layer, what would you tell them today?

**Disconfirming signals (for a NeuroMetric-specific niche)**
- They already offer device-agnostic real-world-use and caregiver modules to neuro-device sponsors, and sponsors are buying them.
- Small-n BCI studies are already well served by a vendor they name.
- Sponsors in this space prefer single-vendor platforms and refuse multi-vendor stacks.
Signals of partnership potential: they turn down small-n or custom device work; they lack neurology-specific measures; they have
no payer-packaging capability.
