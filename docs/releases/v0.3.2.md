**Concord is research software.** It receives data from acquisition systems and recordings;
it never sends anything to a device. It has been tested in software and with
protocol-level test sources only, **never with acquisition hardware or a participant**, and it
is **not a medical device**. It is not for clinical, diagnostic or commercial use. Read [what has and has not been validated](https://github.com/arnavhiteshpatel-cloud/concord/blob/main/docs/VALIDATION_LEVELS.md).

The comparison toolkit alone: `pip install --find-links <folder> concord-hwval`. |
| `SHA256SUMS.txt` | Checking a download | the SHA-256 of every file in this release |

Each bundle was built and smoke-tested on its platform by the release workflow before it was
published (start, stream a simulated source, record, recover from a crash, start and stop a
background server from the command line, open and quit the window; on macOS also the app run
from the mounted `.dmg`, on Windows the app run from the extracted `.zip`). The macOS and
Windows apps have been tested on GitHub's runners only, not yet on a physical Mac or PC.

**Updating from 0.3.1 or 0.3.0:** replace the app. On a Mac, drag the new **Concord** into
Applications and choose **Replace**; on Windows or Linux, extract the new download and use it
instead of the old folder. Your recordings, sessions and settings are in a separate data folder
and are kept.

**Next:** open the app and click **Help** (top right): the in-app guide explains everything,
starting with a two-minute tryout using demo data. The online
[Getting started](https://github.com/arnavhiteshpatel-cloud/concord/blob/main/docs/GETTING_STARTED.md)
guide adds full walkthroughs with real recordings.

## What changed since 0.3.1: a console for first-time users

The console was written for people who already knew the field. It now explains itself.
Nothing about how data is received, processed, decoded or sent changed, and the safety rules
are the same.

- **An in-app guide.** **Help** opens *How Concord works*: what Concord is, a two-minute
  tryout, each step in depth, every tool, the words used, and the limits of use. Each step's
  **What is this?** opens the guide at that topic.
- **A welcome card** with the four steps and **Try it with demo data**, which connects
  Simulated Profile A in one click. **Hide this** hides it for good in that browser.
- **Clear notices of use** on every screen: research preview, not a medical device, not for
  clinical, diagnostic or commercial use, not yet tested with real equipment or people. The
  participant screen says "not a medical device" too.
- **Plain words.** The rows are numbered steps: 1 Data source, 2 Signal check (with a sentence
  saying what the light means), 3 Decoder, 4 Send to apps. Buttons say what they do:
  **Choose a source**, **Train a decoder…**, **Start sending**, **Stop sending**,
  **Stop everything**, **Start a session**.
- **Getting recordings without the online docs.** With no recordings installed, the source list
  gives the two starter downloads and the exact folder to put them in.
- **No more hidden Advanced section.** It is replaced by **More tools**, always visible: every
  tool listed by group with what it does and when to use it, opened with its own **Show**
  button. Pause and playback speed are always visible.
- The guides, glossary and screenshots use the new names. Details are in the
  [changelog](https://github.com/arnavhiteshpatel-cloud/concord/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md).

## What is in this release

- **The operator console** (opens in your browser): connect a source, check the signal, run a
  decoder, train one, authorize applications to receive commands, record, research sessions
  with export and reproduction, stop everything. **Help** explains each part.
- **Sources:** two simulated devices; recorded human data replayed offline (intracortical
  spiking, ECoG, cursor + click); live streams over Lab Streaming Layer and the Open Ephys
  ZMQ Interface; add-ons (BCI2000 included in the app; BrainFlow with the Python install).
- **The communication board**, a participant screen with five input methods.
- **`concord-hwval`**, a toolkit to verify that Concord receives exactly what your
  acquisition software records ([protocol](https://github.com/arnavhiteshpatel-cloud/concord/blob/main/docs/HARDWARE_VALIDATION.md)).

## Known limitations

- Not validated with acquisition hardware or a participant; decoding results are offline and
  open loop only.
- The macOS and Windows apps are unsigned.
- Concord has no user accounts or encryption: it listens only on this computer
  (127.0.0.1); keep it that way.

All limitations: [LIMITATIONS.md](https://github.com/arnavhiteshpatel-cloud/concord/blob/main/LIMITATIONS.md).
